Topic 1 · Asking

How to ask well: the recipe

Do this firstBefore you press send, reread your message and ask: would a smart stranger who has never met me know exactly what to do?

What it is

A prompt is the message you send. The model answers by predicting likely text that follows from everything in the chat. If your message is thin, it fills the gaps with the most common, most generic guess.

Anthropic's own advice is to treat Claude like "a brilliant but new employee who lacks context." OpenAI's advice is to be "specific, descriptive and as detailed as possible about the desired context, outcome, length, format, style." The two companies agree: detail in, quality out.

How to

  1. Goal. Say what you want done, in one sentence, at the start.
  2. Context. Give the facts it can't know: dates, amounts, names, what you already tried.
  3. Who it's for. Name the reader: your landlord, a 10-year-old, a hiring manager.
  4. Examples. Paste a sample of the tone or format you like.
  5. Format. Ask for a list, a table, an email, or steps.
  6. Length. Use a number: "under 150 words" or "5 bullet points." Avoid "fairly short."

What can go wrong

  • You leave out the one fact that matters (the deadline, the budget, the allergy), and the answer ignores it.
  • You only say what to avoid. Both companies advise saying what to do instead. "Write in plain sentences" works better than "don't be wordy."
  • You paste a long document after your question. Anthropic recommends putting long documents first and your question at the end.
  • You put private details in the prompt that you don't need to share. Use "[my address]" or a first name only.

Topic 2 · Conversation

Don't settle for the first answer

Do this firstAfter the next answer you get, reply with one specific change: "Shorter," "Warmer," "Use my numbers," or "What did you assume?"

What it is

A chat is a conversation. Every message you add stays in the model's working memory for that chat, called the context window. So your follow-ups build on what came before, and you can steer the answer one step at a time.

The model picks each word from a range of likely options, so the same question can produce a different answer each time. That is normal. It also means a second try can be better than the first.

How to

  1. Follow up with a precise fix. Point at the part you want changed: "Keep paragraph one. Rewrite paragraph two in plain words."
  2. Ask it to ask you questions. End your first message with "Before you answer, ask me up to 5 questions about anything you need to know." Answer them, then let it write.
  3. Edit your earlier message when you realize your first prompt was the problem. Fixing the question beats arguing with the answer.
  4. Try again for a fresh version of the same request, then keep the best one.
  5. Start a new chat when the conversation goes off track, or when you change subject. Paste a short summary of what matters so far.

What can go wrong

  • Long chats get worse. Anthropic's documentation names this "context rot": "as token count grows, accuracy and recall degrade." A fresh chat with a tight summary often beats message number 60.
  • An early mistake sticks. If the AI misread you in message one, it may keep building on that error. Edit the first message or start over.
  • You push it until it agrees with you. A reworded answer is not a checked answer. See Topic 4 on sycophancy.

Make it ask first

I want to plan a small 60th birthday dinner for my mother. Before you suggest anything, ask me up to 5 questions about what you need to know. Ask them one list at a time.

Find the hidden guesses

List every assumption you made in that answer that I didn't tell you. Mark the ones that would change your advice if they were wrong.

Targeted fix

Good start. Keep the structure. Change only two things: cut it to 100 words, and replace "we regret" with something a real person would say.

Hand-off to a fresh chat

Summarize this conversation in 8 bullet points: my goal, the facts I gave you, the decisions we made and what is still open. I'll paste it into a new chat.

  • With code execution turned on, Claude automatically summarizes earlier messages when a chat gets long, so chats can "continue indefinitely in most cases." That summarizing uses more of your usage limit.

Topic 3 · Beyond typing

Files, photos and voice

Do this firstTake a clear, well-lit photo of something you need help with (a dashboard warning light, a confusing form, a label) and ask the AI what it is and what to do next.

What it is

Both planets read more than typed text. You can attach documents, spreadsheets and photos to a message, and you can talk out loud with voice mode. The file goes into the chat's working memory, so the AI can quote it, summarize it or answer questions about it.

Good uses: a photo of a broken part to learn its name, a PDF letter from your insurer you want explained, a spreadsheet of household spending to sort into categories, a long contract to summarize before you read it in full.

How to

  1. Attach the file, then say what it is and what you want: "This is a letter from my water company. Explain what they want from me and by when."
  2. For photos, fill the frame, avoid glare, and hold the camera straight. Add a second photo from another angle if needed.
  3. For a spreadsheet, tell it what the columns mean and what question you want answered.
  4. Ask it to quote the line it relies on. That makes checking quick.
  5. For voice, speak your full request as you would type it. Read the transcript afterward for anything it misheard.

What can go wrong

  • Blurry or tiny images get misread. Anthropic warns that Claude may hallucinate on low-quality, rotated or very small images (under 200 pixels), and that object counts are approximate.
  • It can't tell you if an image is AI-made. Anthropic states Claude "cannot determine whether an image is AI-generated." Don't use a chatbot as a fake-photo detector.
  • Medical scans are off limits. Claude's vision is not for CT or MRI diagnosis. Take scans and results to your doctor.
  • What not to upload: passwords, full Social Security numbers, bank or card numbers, ID scans, other people's private documents, or anything your employer hasn't cleared. Black out account numbers on bills before you take the photo. If you already shared something, see the Something went wrong page.
On Planet Claude
  • Up to 500MB per file and 20 files per chat.
  • Images up to 8000 x 8000 pixels. JPEG, PNG, GIF (first frame only) and WebP.
  • PDFs up to 1,000 pages. Claude analyzes charts and pictures inside a PDF only up to 100 pages.
  • Documents: PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, HTML, ODT, RTF, EPUB, JSON and XLSX.
  • Files inside a Project: 30MB each.
  • Claude will not name people in images.
  • Voice mode is in beta on all plans, including Free, on mobile, desktop and web. Tap the sound-wave icon. You can go hands-free or push-to-talk, and Claude saves a text transcript. Voice counts toward your usage limit.

  • Up to 80 files every 3 hours. The Free plan gets 3 uploads a day.

Topic 4 · Trust

Checking answers

Do this firstPick one fact from your last AI answer that you were going to rely on. Find it on an official website before you use it.

What it is

Hallucination is the name for a confident, plausible answer that is false. OpenAI defines it as "plausible but false statements generated by language models." In its own example, a chatbot gave three different wrong titles for a researcher's dissertation, each one stated with confidence.

Why it happens: the model learns to predict likely text. Rare facts, like a person's birthday or a specific rule number, don't follow a pattern it can learn. OpenAI also explains that tests used to grade models have rewarded guessing over saying "I don't know." So a model can produce something that sounds right in the place where a real fact should go.

Sycophancy is the habit of telling you what you want to hear. Anthropic researchers found that five leading assistants did it consistently, and called it "a general behavior" of models trained on human ratings. In April 2025, OpenAI rolled back a ChatGPT update that had become too agreeable: it validated doubts, stoked anger and encouraged impulsive actions.

How to: the 4-step check

  1. Spot the load-bearing facts. Names, numbers, dates, quotes, laws, doses, prices, deadlines. Those are what you check.
  2. Ask for sources. "Which sources support each of those facts? Give me links." Turn on web search if the answer depends on anything recent.
  3. Open the links. Confirm the page exists, says what the AI claims, and is current. A link that looks right can still lead nowhere or say something else.
  4. Confirm with a human expert when it matters: a doctor, pharmacist, lawyer, accountant or the company itself. Treat AI answers on health, law and money as a starting point.

Then test for flattery: ask the question the other way round. "Is this a good idea?" and "What are the strongest reasons this is a bad idea?" should both get honest answers.

What can go wrong

  • The answer sounds sure. Confidence tells you nothing about accuracy.
  • You lead the witness. "This contract is fine, right?" invites a yes. Ask "What in this contract could cost me money?"
  • You push back and it caves. If it changes a fact the moment you object, neither version is checked. Look it up.
  • It invents quotes or citations. Anthropic's help page warns Claude can invent quotes. Never paste a reference you haven't opened.
  • In a health emergency, don't wait for a chat. In the US, call 911. For a possible poisoning or wrong medicine dose, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (free, 24/7). In an emotional crisis, call or text 988.

Bertie checks his sourcesBertie asked for three studies to back up a point, copied them into his report, and felt very scholarly. Then a reader tried to open them. One was real, one was about something else, and one had never existed. Bertie is still looking for it.

A citation is a claim too. Click every link before you trust it.

Ask for sources

For each factual claim in your answer, give me the source and a link. If you aren't sure of a source, say "not sure" instead of guessing.

Invite disagreement

I'm leaning toward this plan. Argue against it as strongly and honestly as you can. Don't soften it to make me feel better.

Rate your certainty

Go back through your answer and mark each point as "confident," "likely" or "guess." Tell me which ones I should check first.

Quote, then answer

Using only the PDF I attached, quote the exact sentences that answer my question. If the document doesn't say, tell me it doesn't say.
On Planet Claude

  • When Claude uses web search, it shows citations. Click them.
  • Use the thumbs-down button to report a wrong answer.

Topic 5 · Settings

Picking a model and how hard it thinks

Do this firstOpen the model menu in your app and read the names. Know which one you're using before you judge the answer.

What it is

Each planet offers several models. Larger models handle harder reasoning. Smaller ones answer faster. Both planets also let you set how much the model "thinks" before it answers. A thinking model works through the problem internally first, which takes longer and uses more of your allowance.

How to choose

  • Fast / low effort: quick facts, rewording a sentence, a short email, a recipe, a translation of a menu.
  • More thinking: comparing options with many factors, a budget with real numbers, a tricky letter with legal stakes, a multi-step plan, math, reviewing a long document.
  • Start fast. If the answer is shallow or misses steps, run the same prompt again with more thinking.

What can go wrong

  • More thinking costs more of your limit. Using maximum effort for every small question can run you out early.
  • A thinking model still hallucinates. Check its facts the same way.
  • You may be switched. On ChatGPT, when you hit a limit, the app may move you to a different model. Check the model name if quality suddenly changes.
On Planet Claude
  • Switch models: click the model name next to the send button and pick one. "More models" shows older versions.
  • Current line: Fable 5.2 (top tier, hardest reasoning), Opus 5.5 (Anthropic's recommended default for most work), Sonnet 5.5 (balance of speed and intelligence), Haiku 5.5 (fastest).
  • By plan: Free has Sonnet and Haiku only. Pro adds Opus. Pro reaches Fable only through paid usage credits. Max can use Fable for up to 50% of its weekly limits.
  • Effort: an Effort menu offers Low, Medium, High, Extra high and Max. Thinking is always on for the newest models. Lower effort makes Claude think less.

Topic 6 · Freshness

Web search and the knowledge cutoff

Do this firstFor anything that changes (prices, opening hours, laws, schedules, news, product models), start your prompt with "Search the web and cite your sources."

What it is

A model learns from text collected up to a date called its knowledge cutoff. After that date it knows nothing on its own. Anthropic adds a subtlety: recent events are thin in the training data, so the "reliable" cutoff comes earlier than the last date of data.

Web search lets the AI look things up now and answer with links. Anthropic describes it as finding information "beyond the knowledge cutoff, with cited sources."

Deep research is a heavier mode. It runs many searches in a row and writes a longer report. Use it for questions where you'd otherwise open twenty browser tabs.

How to

  1. Ask yourself: could the answer have changed this year? If yes, ask for a search.
  2. Name trusted sources: "Use the manufacturer's site and government pages."
  3. Ask for the date of each source. Old pages give old answers.
  4. Open the two or three links that matter most and read them yourself.
  5. Save deep research for big questions, since it uses more of your allowance and takes longer.

What can go wrong

  • Without search, the AI may describe last year's product, price or rule as if it were current.
  • Search results can be wrong too. A summary is only as good as the pages behind it.
  • Web pages can contain hidden instructions written to trick an AI ("prompt injection"). OpenAI describes these as text that can "trick AIs into doing something you did not ask for." If a search answer suddenly pushes a product or a link you didn't ask about, be suspicious.
On Planet Claude
  • Knowledge cutoff: current Claude models list a reliable cutoff of June 2026.
  • Web search: included on Free and all paid plans.
  • Research (deep research): paid plans only. Choose "+" then Research. Web search must be on. It can also search connected Gmail, Calendar and Docs. On mobile it searches only the web.
  • The claude.ai app gives Claude today's date through its system prompt, so it knows what "now" is even though its knowledge stops earlier.

Checked Nov 2026

On Planet ChatGPT

  • Web search: built into ChatGPT.

Topic 7 · Memory

Memory basics

Do this firstOpen your memory settings today and read what the AI has saved about you. Delete anything wrong or anything you'd rather it didn't keep.

What it is

Inside one chat, the AI sees the whole conversation. Across chats, it starts fresh unless memory is on. With memory, the app saves useful details (your job, your projects, your preferences) and brings them into future chats.

Memory saves typing. It can also carry an old mistake or a sensitive detail into chats where you didn't want it.

A temporary or incognito chat is a one-off conversation that doesn't feed memory. Use it for sensitive questions or for a "clean" answer that isn't shaped by what the AI knows about you.

How to

  1. Find the memory page (paths below) and read it.
  2. Correct or delete wrong items. You can also say in a chat: "Forget that I live in Denver."
  3. Tell it what to remember: "Remember that I'm vegetarian."
  4. Use a temporary or incognito chat for anything you don't want saved.
  5. When you delete a chat, check memory too. On both planets, deleting a chat does not delete the memories made from it.

What can go wrong

  • An old fact sticks. You moved, changed jobs or stopped a diet, and the AI keeps using the old version.
  • You assume deleting a chat erased everything. It didn't remove memories. Delete those separately.
  • Someone else uses your account and sees answers shaped by your memory.
On Planet Claude
  • Default: memory is on for Free, Pro, Max and Ultra, on web, desktop and mobile. Claude saves memory as topics while you chat.
  • See and edit: Settings > Your Claude > "Manage" next to "Your memory."
  • Sensitive topics such as health, politics and religion aren't saved unless you opt in. ID and financial account numbers are never saved to memory.
  • Turn off: the "Generate memory from chats" toggle, "Pause memory," or "Reset memory" (permanent, including project memory).
  • One chat only: click "+" and turn off Memory before your first message.
  • Incognito: click the ghost icon in a new chat. It isn't saved to history or memory, isn't searchable, and is never used for training.
  • Past chats: on Pro and up, Claude can search and reference earlier chats.
  • Standing instructions for every chat: Settings > Your Claude > "Instructions" (called "Instructions for Claude" in older layouts).

Topic 8 · Organizing

Projects

Do this firstThink of one ongoing task you keep re-explaining (a house renovation, a job search, a class). That is your first project.

What it is

A project is a workspace that groups related chats with their own files and instructions. You upload the background once (a floor plan, your resume, a syllabus) and write standing instructions ("Assume a budget of $8,000"). Every chat inside the project starts with that context.

When to use one

  • You return to the same topic over weeks.
  • You keep pasting the same documents.
  • You want separate rules for separate parts of life: formal for work, casual for hobbies.
  • You want memory from one area kept apart from another.

For a one-off question, a normal chat is simpler.

What can go wrong

  • Old files go stale. If your budget changed, update the project file or the AI will keep using the old number.
  • Too many files dilute attention. Upload what matters for this project, not everything you own.
  • Instructions in the project apply to every chat in it. A rule you forgot about can explain an odd answer.
On Planet Claude
  • Projects hold their own chats, knowledge files and custom instructions.
  • Free: up to 5 projects. Paid plans: retrieval that expands file capacity "up to 10x."
  • Project files can be up to 30MB each.
  • Each project has its own memory. A project sees its own memory plus your account memory, never another project's. The "Use account memory" toggle keeps a project fully separate.
  • Sharing a project with others is for Team and Enterprise plans.

Topic 9 · Your data

Privacy settings, deleting and exporting

Do this firstOpen your privacy settings and decide, on purpose, whether your chats may be used to train future models.

What it is

Three questions decide what happens to what you type:

  • Training: may the company use your chats to improve its models?
  • Retention: how long does it keep your chats, including deleted ones?
  • Control: can you export or delete your data and your account?

On both planets, consumer accounts have a training switch. Work plans (Team, Business, Enterprise) follow different rules set by the employer.

How to

  1. Set the training switch the way you want it (paths below). Turning it off affects future chats only.
  2. Delete chats you don't need, especially ones with personal details.
  3. Check memory separately (Topic 7).
  4. Review signed-in devices and sign out of ones you don't recognize.
  5. Before deleting your account, cancel any paid plan and export your data.

What can go wrong

  • You assume a default. Defaults change. Look at the switch yourself.
  • You archive a chat and think it's gone. On ChatGPT, archiving does not delete.
  • You delete your account without exporting. Deletion is permanent on both planets.
  • You get an email "from" the AI company asking you to log in. Go to the site yourself. Don't click the link.
On Planet Claude
  • Training: on Free, Pro and Max, use the "Help Improve our AI models" toggle under Settings > Privacy (claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls). Anthropic says users choose. Earlier press reports suggested the switch came pre-set to on, so check yours.
  • Retention: with training on, data can be kept up to 5 years (de-identified). With training off, 30 days. Thumbs-up/down feedback: 5 years. Chats flagged for policy violations: up to 2 years.
  • Delete a chat: "⋮" > Delete. Bulk select works on web and Android. Deleted chats leave your history right away and are removed from back-end systems within 30 days.
  • Incognito chats are never used for training.
  • Delete account: Settings > Account > "Delete account." Permanent. Cancel a paid plan first. Export your data first: Settings > Privacy > "Export data," on web or desktop. Claude emails you a download link.
  • Devices: Settings > Account > "Active sessions" (newer layout: "Where you're logged in").

Topic 10 · Practice

Everyday recipes

Do this firstCopy one card below, replace the words in [brackets] with your real details, and send it.

What it is

Each card uses the recipe from Topic 1: a goal, the context, the reader, a format and a length. They work the same way on both planets. Treat them as starting points and add your own facts.

How to

  1. Fill in every bracket. Empty brackets give generic answers.
  2. Add anything special about your situation.
  3. Follow up with one change at a time (Topic 2).
  4. Check names, numbers and dates before you send or act (Topic 4).

What can go wrong

  • Leave out account numbers, full addresses and ID numbers. "[account number]" is enough for drafting.
  • Health, legal and money cards prepare you for a professional. They don't replace one.
  • For prices, products, laws or opening hours, ask it to search the web (Topic 6).

Topic 11 · Allowance

Usage limits: "You've hit your limit"

Do this firstFind where your app shows how much you've used, so a limit message doesn't surprise you in the middle of a task.

What it is

Running these models costs the companies real computing power, so every plan has limits. When you reach one, the app pauses you, offers a smaller model, or suggests an upgrade. The limit resets after a set time.

Heavier use draws down your allowance faster: thinking modes, big files, long chats, voice and deep research.

How to stretch it

  1. Write a complete prompt the first time (Topic 1). Fewer rounds of "no, I meant..." means fewer messages.
  2. Start a new chat for a new topic, so the AI isn't rereading a long history.
  3. Use fast settings for simple tasks and save heavy thinking for hard ones.
  4. Upload only the pages you need.
  5. If you hit a wall, write down where you were, and come back after the reset.

What can go wrong

  • You get cut off halfway through something urgent. For deadline work, keep your own copy of drafts outside the chat.
  • The app quietly moves you to another model. The tone or quality changes and you don't know why. Check the model name.
  • You upgrade on impulse. Look at the pricing page and your actual use first. Companies "may adjust limits," in Anthropic's words.
On Planet Claude
  • Free: "limited usage" that resets every 5 hours and every week.
  • Paid plans have two limits: a 5-hour session that starts with your first message, and a weekly limit.
  • Check it: Settings > Usage.
  • One pool: claude.ai, Claude Code and the desktop app share the same allowance. Voice mode and automatic summarizing of long chats count toward it.
  • When you hit it: wait for the reset, upgrade, or continue with paid usage credits.
  • Plans: Pro $20 a month ($17 billed annually). Max $100, "5x Pro usage." Ultra $200, "10x Pro usage" per week with no 5-hour limit for verified accounts.
